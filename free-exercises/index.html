Two short exercises you can try on your own.

The questions you don’t ask yourself

My questions are what people mention most. Pick a situation and see the kind of questions I’d ask.

“Should I change jobs?”

  1. Why are you considering this in the first place?

    Was a certain value broken in your work environment that made you question your worth there? Or are you looking for a new challenge to keep you motivated and feeling worthy?

  2. If you had all the money you wanted, would you still leave?

    What would you really like to do?

  3. Is it really you who wants to leave, or are you being pressured by others to do what they think is best for you?

“I keep putting things off.”

  1. When exactly do you decide you’re not going to do a specific task?

  2. What heavy expectations, from yourself or others, make your tasks harder to complete?

  3. Who said it has to be perfect, and tied your worth to its perfection?

“I feel behind everyone.”

  1. Behind who, exactly?

  2. Whose timeline are you measuring yourself against?

  3. If no one could see your progress, and you couldn’t see anyone else’s, how would you judge what you’ve achieved?

“I can’t stop overthinking.”

  1. What are you protecting yourself from by thinking more?

  2. What’s the worst thing that could happen if you stopped overthinking and acted?

    What has overthinking cost you until now? Is it worth it?

  3. Imagine there could never be a wrong outcome, and you trusted yourself and your judgement. What would you act on today?

“I always put others first.”

  1. What are you afraid will happen if you say no?

  2. What do you get from always being needed?

  3. What would happen if you treated yourself the way you treat others?

“My business isn’t growing.”

  1. Which decision have you been postponing the longest?

  2. What are you still doing yourself that someone or something else could do?

  3. What does growth mean to you, aside from revenue?

These are only starting points. In a session, my questions follow your words and your patterns, wherever they lead.

Try it: say it more precisely

“I’m worried.” “I’m stressed.” “I don’t know what I feel.” Vague words keep us stuck, because we can’t act on them. Try finding a more precise word.

Which word sounds most like you right now?

Which of these is closest? (pick one, or “none of these”)

You started with “.” Now you have: “.”

Notice how different that feels. The second sentence shows you where to look.
